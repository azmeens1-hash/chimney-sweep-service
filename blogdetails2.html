How Often Should You Really Get a Chimney Inspection?

By Arjun Varma Sep 3, 2026 5 min read

"Once a year" is the answer most people expect, and it's not wrong — but it's also not the whole picture. How often your chimney actually needs a camera-assisted inspection depends less on the calendar and more on how the fireplace gets used.

It depends on how often you burn

A fireplace used a handful of times a winter wears very differently from one that's lit most evenings from November through February. If you're burning heavily, an inspection at the start of every season — not just once a year — catches problems while they're still small.

Buying a home with an existing fireplace

If you've just moved into a home with a chimney you've never had checked, treat that as its own trigger regardless of when the last inspection happened. You have no way of knowing what the previous owner did — or didn't — maintain.

"An inspection is cheap insurance against a liner crack that's still small enough to be a simple fix."

What a camera actually catches

A flexible camera run down the flue reveals things a flashlight from the firebox never will — hairline cracks partway up the liner, gaps in mortar joints, or a bird's nest tucked just out of sight. These are exactly the issues that turn expensive if they're missed for another year.

A simple rule of thumb

Light use, well-maintained chimney: once a year is genuinely enough. Heavy use, an older chimney, or you can't remember the last inspection: book one now rather than waiting for the "right" time on the calendar.

Either way, an inspection ends with a written report — so the next decision about your chimney is based on what we actually found, not a guess.
